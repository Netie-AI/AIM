  gradients                     13.04 GB             -         --

  TOTAL                        120.31 GB      22.88 GB         5x

  Honest split of that 5.3x: the parameter side (104 GB -> 23 GB) is only 4.6x.
  The activation side is 65,536x.  Deleting backprop-through-time is
  where the memory win lives; low-precision weights are a rounding
  error by comparison. The plan under-sells one and over-sells the other.

E4.3  per-step inter-device traffic
==============================================================================
  data-parallel ring allreduce:      26.08 GB/step
  4096-lane credit bus:              16.00 KB/step
  ratio:                         1,708,984x
  link energy per step:              0.448 J   vs 0.262 uJ

  This is the largest ratio in the entire design and the one that
  justifies deleting the interconnect fabric. It is also the claim
  most exposed to E2: the bus only works if it has enough lanes.

==============================================================================
E4.4  does the experience store fit on an SSD?
==============================================================================
       100,000 episodes x 64 steps ->    0.02 TB   NAND $         1   HBM $          372
     1,000,000 episodes x 64 steps ->    0.24 TB   NAND $        15   HBM $        3,719
    10,000,000 episodes x 64 steps ->    2.42 TB   NAND $       149   HBM $       37,193

  feeding 100,000 replayed steps/s needs 0.42 GB/s; one NVMe delivers 7 GB/s
  -> 17x headroom. The replay path is genuinely
     SSD-shaped: append-mostly, read-sequential, cold, and huge.
  -> and 250x cheaper per byte than HBM.

==============================================================================
E4.5  can the WEIGHTS live on the SSD too?
==============================================================================
  HBM          4,800 GB/s -> break-even batch           10 tokens/step
  DRAM           400 GB/s -> break-even batch          125 tokens/step
  NVMe SSD         7 GB/s -> break-even batch        7,143 tokens/step

  Weight streaming from NAND needs a batch three orders of magnitude
  larger than from HBM to stay compute-bound. For batched offline
  training that is reachable; for single-agent online adaptation it
  is not. So: experience on SSD yes, weights on SSD only in batch.
  The weights want to be IN the compute, which is the actual argument
  for compute-in-memory -- not density, but the deletion of this row.

==============================================================================
E4.6  the constraint the plan creates but does not budget for
==============================================================================
  Traces are read-modify-written once per weight per step. That is the
  single worst access pattern for any tier except the one the weights
  already sit in. It cannot go on NAND (endurance) and should not go
  on HBM (that is the row we were deleting). It has to be on-die.
  So on-die trace SRAM, not FLOPs, sets how large a model a die can adapt.

   on-die SRAM   adapted params   as % of 7B   array area*
         64 MB            22 M        0.32%         28 mm2
        256 MB            89 M        1.28%        113 mm2
      1,024 MB           358 M        5.11%        451 mm2

  * array area only, at a 5nm-class 6T cell; excludes compute tiles,
    I/O and the DRAM/NVM controllers.

  This closes the design. A reticle-scale die can hold traces for
  roughly 10^8 adapted parameters, not 10^10. The RPU is therefore not
  a from-scratch pretraining chip -- it is a sparse-adaptation engine:
  a large frozen low-precision backbone held in cheap memory, plus a
  ~100M-parameter adapted surface whose traces live on-die. That is a
  narrower and much more defensible product than the original framing,
  and it is the only version the arithmetic actually supports.
