  exponent at widths 16-128:      0.80
  exponent at widths 1k-65k:      1.01

  The exponent DRIFTS UP with scale toward 1.0, which is the
  asymptote perturbation theory predicts: a scalar shared by N units
  costs O(N). E2's 0.80, fitted at widths 16-128, was optimistic --
  small-scale fits understate the penalty. Design against 1.0.

  Consequences: one wire across 65k units is 55,000x an exact
  gradient, so the single-scalar bus is dead beyond argument. But
  lanes still rescue it -- holding slowdown near 10x needs ~10 units
  per lane, so ~10^4 lanes for a 10^5-unit adapted surface. At 4 B per
  lane that is 40 KB/step against 26.08 GB for an allreduce: still a
  ~650,000x saving. The bus survives a linear exponent; it just has
  to be two orders of magnitude wider than 4096.
